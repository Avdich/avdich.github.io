The longer story

I started as a graphic designer — print work, brand systems, the craft of getting things to look right on paper. Then the web got interesting, and I taught myself to build what I was designing. An MFA in Media & Art Studies gave me a framework for thinking critically about visual communication — not just how to make things look good, but why certain visual choices land and others don't.

Most designers hand off to a developer and hope for the best. Most developers build what they're given. I do both, which means fewer surprises at the end of a project and a much shorter path from idea to live product.

These days I'm drawn to projects where visual craft matters — illustration-heavy interfaces, brand-forward marketing sites, design systems that a team actually wants to use. If it needs to look good and be technically solid, that's the brief I like.
